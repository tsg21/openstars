# 19. Diplomacy and Trade

Multi-player games only.

Stars! has features that allow you to practice diplomacy in multi-player games. Use the mail feature of the Message pane to communicate with other players, arranging alliances, rendezvous, trade agreements, joint remote mining ventures, and non-aggression pacts. Stars! mail works equally well for hurling insults and taunts.

Stars! does not require a single winner. When you set up the game, you can specify winning conditions that encourage diplomacy by allowing multiple winners.

## Player Relationships

You can declare neutral and friendly players using the `Player Relations` dialog. This prevents you from attacking the wrong people and allows you to automatically come to a friend's aid if they're under fire.

Friends can also:

- pass through each other's mine fields without harm
- automatically refuel at each other's starbases
- use each other's stargates

It is worthwhile to set up alliances, even temporary ones.

## Trading Fuel and Minerals

You can trade fuel and minerals with other players by sending messages to arrange the trade, then rendezvousing with the other player's fleet to give or receive cargo.

Minerals can also be sent to another player's planet:

- by using a fleet-to-planet transfer
- by flinging mineral packets at the planet

Packet delivery assumes the receiving planet has a mass driver of equal or greater strength. Otherwise, you may have started a war.

Fleet-based trade can be automated by setting waypoints between planets or fleets, specifying `Transport` orders, and then selecting `Repeat Orders`.

Mineral packet trade can be automated by adding `Mineral Packets (Auto Build)` to your production queue and aiming the mass driver at the correct planet.

## Trading Technology

You can trade technological research data by scrapping fleets at each other's starbases. This is not always 100% successful because races have different underlying understandings of physics, but it can add useful depth to an alliance.

## Trading Ships

You can give fleets to other players by using the `Transfer Fleets` waypoint task.

The other player can take the fleet only if doing so would not cause them to exceed the maximum number of ship designs. The receiving player cannot build additional ships of the types given by other players, but they can receive more ships of the same type from the original owner.

## Trading with Transdimensional Beings

You may encounter a transdimensional being with technology to sell during the course of a game. If you can catch this Mystery Trader and give it what it wants, you may learn technology that neither you nor any other player would normally be able to research or build.

## Joint Mining Ventures

You can cooperate with other players to remote mine a planet. Each player can use their own remote-mining fleet and freighters, or one player can mine and then manually transfer the minerals to the other player's cargo holds for transport to the agreed ports of call.

Any Super Stealth race can automate cargo transfer from another player's mining fleet as a waypoint task. If the other player agrees, it's diplomacy. Otherwise, it's piracy.

## Claim Adjusters and Orbital Terraforming

Races with the `Claim Adjuster` trait can terraform other players' planets from orbit. This creates opportunities for either diplomacy or war.

- If the owner is your Friend, you perform positive terraforming, adjusting conditions toward the inhabitants' ideal environment.
- If the owner is your Enemy, you perform negative terraforming.

Terraforming from orbit happens automatically as soon as your fleet arrives. Just set the destination planet as the fleet waypoint. No waypoint task is required.

### Terraforming for Hire

Terraforming from orbit requires a fleet fitted with `Orbital Adjusters`, described in the Mining Robots section of the `Technology Browser`. Every race with the `Claim Adjuster` trait starts with one ship fitted with `Orbital Adjusters`.

Terraforming from orbit can also be used diplomatically. In exchange for technology, an alliance, or even a simple thank-you, you can help friendly races improve their planets. This is most useful when your ally has weaker terraforming capability than you do.

Terraforming is not additive. You cannot combine your orbital terraforming ability with the inhabitants' terraforming ability to exceed the stronger limit. The planet is terraformed only to the limit of whoever has the superior technology.

For example, if the inhabitants have 3% terraforming and you have 5%, the planet can be improved by up to 5% from its original conditions, without exceeding the inhabitants' optimal conditions.
