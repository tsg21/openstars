Minerals

There are three types of minerals: Ironium, Boranium, Germanium.

Each of these minerals is equally likely to be found on each of the planets. When creating a galaxy at the beginning of the game, each planet is given a mineral concentration value. Most often this number is from 1 to 100, but it can vary from 1 to 200. A given planet may have a lot of one mineral but little of another. There may be no minerals at all or there may be a lot of them all.

From the point of view of minerals, the planet is characterized by both the concentration of minerals (3 natural numbers characterizing the concentration for each mineral) and their presence on the surface of the planet (3 non-negative integer numbers in kt). At the beginning of the game, all uninhabited planets do not have any kt minerals on their surface.

During the mining process, minerals are removed from the planet and appear on the surface. At the same time, depending on the rate of extraction, the concentration of minerals on the planet gradually decreases.

Minerals on the surface can already be used at your discretion - used in the production of ships, orbital stations, factories, radar or protective devices. They can also be picked up and taken to another planet, or packaged in a mineral package and launched into another planet.

Each type of mineral is used for different purposes.

Ironium is the rarest and most needed mineral in the game. It is used to build ship hulls, missiles and torpedoes, and mineral mining robots.

Boranium is the least used mineral. It is necessary for beam weapons and bombs, for which it is usually more than enough.

Germanium is a highly desirable mineral for most races in the early and mid game due to the fact that it is required to build factories, which affects the productivity of planets. In the final stage of the game it is not of decisive importance and is used for the production of various electronics.

Minerals can be made from nothing using alchemy. It lies in the fact that at any moment a planet can spend 100 (or 25 if MA is selected) resources and receive 1 kt of each mineral for it. This is an extremely costly process and is recommended to be avoided at all costs.

Minerals are mined either by mines or by robots on distant planets. This accounts for 99% of all loot in the game. But you might get lucky and a comet will arrive, which will cause a small Armageddon in the case of colonists, but at the same time increase the concentration of minerals and bring minerals directly to the surface of the planet.

When mining minerals with robots, one fleet of robots should not exceed a capacity of more than 4,000 mines/year. Otherwise, if there is an excess, then the entire group digs exactly 4000 mines/year. To fix the problem, you can break the given 4000+ fleet into smaller fleets that have a production capacity of 4000 or less.

In outer space, as a result of battles and scrapping, minerals remain in the form of some kind of debris (salvage). Their composition is determined entirely by the ships and the cargo from which they were formed, and they also degrade over time (at a rate of 10% per year and at least 10 kt of each mineral per year), therefore, if needed, it is advisable to quickly pick them up by transport.
