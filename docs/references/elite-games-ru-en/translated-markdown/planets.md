Planets

Planets are scattered throughout the galaxy and are visible on the map as dots. Perhaps the creators of Stars! meant that each star has one planet, or in Stars! Only habitable planets are shown, but the fact is that one dot means one planet that is habitable.

Each of the planets has its own horizontal and vertical coordinates. These coordinates never change during the game.

Possible images of planets:

The names of the planets are strictly stitched inside Stars!. In particular, the authors (McBride and Johnson) are immortalized there, all the planets of the solar system are indicated, star systems are present (Alpha Centauru, Vega), the appearance of whists (Vista) and a number of other groups of vocabulary words are predicted.

The planet at any given time is characterized by the following parameters:

Climate

Gravity, temperature and radiation values. There are two values ​​for each of the parameters: current and native.

Belonging to one of the races

The planet can be either uninhabited or inhabited by only one of the races.

Number of people on the planet

This parameter indicates how many representatives of the race live on the planet at the moment.

Mineral concentration

The concentration of each of the three minerals in the interior of the planet. This parameter determines how efficiently the mines or robots that extract minerals will operate.

Presence of minerals on the surface of the planet

Shows how many of each type of mineral are present on the planet's surface. Minerals located on the surface can be used or loaded/unloaded at any time.

Number of factories

If there are colonists on the planet, then these factories increase their productivity according to the economic parameters of the race.

Number of mines

If there are colonists on the planet, then the mines produce minerals according to the economic parameters of the race.

Number of protective structures

These structures (defenses) protect planetary buildings and the population from enemy bombing, landings and attacks from mineral packets.

Availability of planetary radar

Radar on a planet is either present or absent. If it is present, then its ability to scan is completely determined by the level of development of the race's technology (mainly electronics), as well as the value of the NAS.

Availability and design of orbital station

An orbital station may or may not be present.
